## Tracked threads — moved or not

- **Synthetic media and the midterms (today, 3 Nov): the count and spend of AI political ads kept climbing; the mode is openly fake "slopaganda", not deception** — AFP via New Straits Times (Anuj Chopra, 27 Oct 2026): the Wesleyan Media Project has identified 220 AI-generated or AI-assisted political ads nationwide costing more than US$137 million by its estimate, against under US$50,000 in the 2024 cycle; 81% Republican-sponsored, 12% Democratic; only 27% carried an AI disclaimer; Wesleyan says the count is "almost certainly an undercount". Three weeks earlier Al Jazeera (Saumya Roy, 9 Oct 2026) had the same tracker at over 164 ads and over $80 million, so the series moved. "Slopaganda" is Scott Babwah Brennen's term (NYU Center on Technology Policy, co-author of a report "Political Slopaganda"): ads that are identifiably fake and aim to "communicate deeper truths rather than factual truths" (his words, Al Jazeera). AAPC Foundation survey: 73% found AI imagery in campaign ads "unacceptable", 95% for fabricated opponent speech. Examples across the coverage: Senate Leadership Fund's Ossoff salon ad (no disclosure; Ossoff called such deepfakes "deeply troubling" on C-SPAN), Blakeman's Hochul/Mamdani gardening ad (labelled; New York Democrats filed a complaint), Talarico in a dress (Citizens for Sanity, per Garbage Day 2 Nov). Counter-reading: Platformer's "The AI election that wasn't" (Casey Newton, 27 Oct 2026) — paywalled, subtitle only: "Voters have other things on their minds — for now", plus "OpenAI's clever policy to fight deepfakes". CBS News aired "Political AI deepfakes infiltrating midterm elections, could pose risks to voters" on 2 Nov 2026 (headline only). No Election Day deepfake incident found as of this gather.
