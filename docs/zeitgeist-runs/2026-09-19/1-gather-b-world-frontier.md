## 4. Scientific

- **NASA orbiter identifies a large, newly formed lunar crater** — NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter team reported a crater about 728 ft (222 m) wide and up to 141 ft (43 m) deep that formed between 11 April and 22 May 2024, described as the largest newly formed crater yet identified; an impact of this scale is estimated at roughly once every 132 years. It was found in October 2025 during a routine data-quality check by Robert Wagner of the LRO camera team; named McGetchin crater. (source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260917003715.htm ; https://www.space.com/astronomy/moon/nasa-just-found-a-giant-new-crater-on-the-moon-heres-why-everyones-talking-the-once-in-a-century-discovery)
- **New geophysical evidence for possible hidden spaces beside Tutankhamun's tomb** — A team led by Mamdouh Eldamaty (Ain Shams University) combined ground-penetrating radar with a microgravity survey (over 1,200 measurements across about 35 m²) and reports signs of a possible rubble-filled corridor and a network extending some 25–30 m north of the burial chamber; reported as a Nature news exclusive, online 17 September. Outside specialists say further evidence is needed; small-bore camera inspection could follow as early as November 2026 if permission is granted. (source: https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02621-2 ; https://www.heritagedaily.com/2026/09/new-evidence-points-to-possible-hidden-chambers-beyond-tutankhamuns-tomb/159329)
- **Controlled muonium beam opens a route to testing gravity on exotic matter** — ETH Zurich and Paul Scherrer Institute researchers (lead author Jesse Zhang; Prof. A. Soter) report in Nature Physics a method for producing a controlled beam of muonium. The gravity measurement itself has not been done: the team hopes to test the method with the beam this year, with the actual gravity experiment two to three years out if that succeeds; they say a fifth-force search is not the main objective. (source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260918024808.htm)
- **Cells that start programmed death, survive, and rebuild tissue** — Weizmann Institute researchers report in Nature Communications (release dated 18 Sept) that certain cells in fruit fly larvae initiate the cell-death pathway but survive and rapidly rebuild damaged tissue, with descendants reported as about seven times more resistant to cell death. Fruit flies only; the authors say more work is needed to know how far this applies to humans, and note the same mechanism could help cancer cells evade treatment. (source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260917003722.htm)
- **Also circulating, with caveats** — (a) NASA says the Roman Space Telescope may have fuel for at least 22 years, more than double its 10-year design life (source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260918024802.htm). (b) A Nature Human Behaviour study (15 Sept) of 12,247 people in 100 cultures argues collectivism is better measured as duties in relationships (source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02525-1). (c) RECYCLED: the "Menin reverses ageing in mice" story on ScienceDaily's 18 Sept front page traces to a Xiamen University PLOS Biology paper from March 2023, mice only, authors explicitly not claiming a human anti-ageing effect (source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260918000437.htm). ## 5. Technological

- **Frontier-lab chiefs publicly back slowing the pace of AI development** — On 12 Sept Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay, "We Must Pace the Frontier", proposing embedded independent evaluators, common safety standards among democracies, and eventual international limits on capabilities such as recursive self-improvement; Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly agreed. Amodei said development would still be "relatively fast" — a pacing, not a halt. The Washington Post reportedly followed with a story that Anthropic, OpenAI and Google have privately discussed a new safety body. This followed the 8 Sept resignation of Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon, whose public warning drew very large viewership on X (outlets cite figures from ~76 million overnight to over 150 million). (source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/anthropic-ceo-says-it-s-time-to-slow-pace-of-improving-ai-models ; https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/09/12/anthropic-ceo-calls-for-ai-race-to-slow-down-musk-and-openai-s-altman-agrees ; https://time.com/article/2026/09/15/ai-anthropic-researcher-quits-coxon-slowdown/)
- **Antitrust class action filed over the alleged "slowdown pact"** — On Friday 18 Sept four paying subscribers filed a proposed class action in the US District Court for the Northern District of California against Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google, alleging that a coordinated slowdown violates Section 1 of the Sherman Act; they seek class certification, an injunction and declaratory judgment. These are allegations in a filing, not findings. (source: https://www.local10.com/business/2026/09/19/lawsuit-says-anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-made-illegal-agreement-on-ai-slowdown/ ; https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/openai-anthropic-google-spacexai-hit-with-antitrust-lawsuit)

Gavin Newsom signed an executive order directing an expert working group to deliver, within two months, recommendations including a possible required "kill switch" for frontier models and onsite independent verification organisations auditing frontier labs. These are recommendations to be developed, not yet binding requirements. On 19 Sept President Trump said on Truth Social he would create an "AI Force" modelled on the Space Force and name an "AI czar"; few details on remit, budget or timing were given. (source: https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-executive-order-to-accelerate-independent-oversight-and-advance-the-creation-of-an-ai-kill-switch/ ; https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/california-newsom-executive-order-ai.html ; https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/19/politics/trump-ai-task-force-czar)
