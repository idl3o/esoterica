## Ecological

- **Copernicus: second-warmest September, record ocean surface and record Niño 3.4** — The C3S bulletin of 12 October puts September 2026 global surface air at 16.27°C, 0.82°C above 1991–2020 and 1.63°C above 1850–1900, 0.11°C cooler than September 2023 and the second consecutive month above 1.5°C; extra-polar SST 21.05°C, the highest September on record (previous 20.92°C in 2023); Niño 3.4 SST 29.70°C, exceeding the 1982/83, 1997/98 and 2015/16 peaks; Antarctic September sea-ice extent third lowest (about 6% below average). C3S director Carlo Buontempo: "Crossing the Paris Agreement's long-term limit is only a matter of time." (source: https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-second-warmest-september-global-temperatures-stay-above-15degc)
- **El Niño: NOAA gives an 83% chance of a record October–December; WMO projects roughly +3.7°C** — NOAA CPC's ENSO discussion of 8 October keeps the El Niño Advisory, gives September Niño-3.4 at +2.1°C, a "greater than an 83% chance" of strong-to-very-strong El Niño through January–March 2027, and an 83% chance that October–December exceeds the +2.5°C three-month RONI threshold for a record event. WMO's update of 8 October gives August Niño 3.4 at +2.5°C above normal and forecasts October–December at "approximately +3.7°C" (a model projection, not an observation), which would exceed the +2.6°C record of November 2015–January 2016; Secretary-General Celeste Saulo: "El Niño is firmly established and strengthening rapidly." (sources: https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml ; https://wmo.int/node/39068)
