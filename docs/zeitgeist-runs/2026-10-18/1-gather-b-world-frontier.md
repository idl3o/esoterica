## 4. Scientific

- **Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine awarded for optogenetics** — Announced 5 Oct 2026 by the Nobel Assembly at Karolinska Institutet: Karl Deisseroth (Stanford, 54), Peter Hegemann (71) and Georg Nagel (73) share the prize "for their discoveries concerning light-activated ion channels and optogenetics" (citation as carried by Al Jazeera, 5 Oct). Hegemann and Nagel's work identified channelrhodopsin in a single-celled alga; Deisseroth turned the protein into a light-controlled switch for nerve cells. Prize 12 million Swedish kronor, shared. Happened 5 Oct; published 5 Oct. (source: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/5/nobel-medicine-prize-honours-us-and-german-scientists-for-optogenetics-work)
- **Physics Nobel to Francis Halzen for IceCube; chemistry to Kagan and Soai** — 6 Oct: the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the physics prize to Belgian-American physicist Francis Halzen for "decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin" (Reuters via Daily Maverick, 6 Oct; the report names Halzen alone). 7 Oct: the chemistry prize went to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for discovering non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis (Khaleej Times, 7 Oct). Each prize is 12 million Swedish crowns, about $1.2m per Reuters. Happened 6–7 Oct; published 6–7 Oct. (source: https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-06-francis-halzen-wins-2026-nobel-prize-in-physics.md ; https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/henri-kagan-kenso-soai-win-2026-nobel-chemistry-prize)
- **USGS: deep-sea sulphide rocks spontaneously ignited in the laboratory** — US Geological Survey researchers report in Scientific Reports that two of 57 hydrothermal rock samples, collected by remotely operated vehicle in 2022 from the Escanaba Trough off the Oregon–California border, spontaneously combusted after freeze-drying, reaching temperatures above 100 °C; the burning samples were mostly nanocrystalline marcasite, an iron sulphide (USGS figures). USGS frames the finding as a shipboard fire hazard for future seafloor massive-sulphide mining. Samples collected 2022; USGS publications page dated 28 Sep 2026; news release reported 7 Oct; trade and general press carried it 15–16 Oct (Northern Miner, 16 Oct, paywalled beyond the first paragraph). (source: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/cmhrp/news/deep-sea-sulfide-minerals-can-spontaneously-heat-and-combust-when-exposed-air ; https://www.northernminer.com/regulatory-issues/seabed-rocks-catch-fire-during-lab-tests/1003895804/)
- **"BioPykrete": an ice composite about ten times stronger than ice** — A Hebrew University of Jerusalem team under Prof. Ido Braslavsky reports a composite of ice, plant-derived cellulose nanocrystals and an engineered protein with "about" ten times the compressive strength of pure ice and "around" 70 times the energy absorption before failure; adding the protein roughly doubled both figures against the same mix without it (researchers' figures, in Colloids and Surfaces B: Biointerfaces). The "close to concrete" comparison is for compression only, on roughly 2 cm lab samples; the researchers call it a proof of concept still needing freeze–thaw and creep testing. Braslavsky, quoted by Ynetnews: "Instead of shattering suddenly, the material can absorb a large amount of energy and deform gradually." Journal date not stated on any page seen; press coverage began 26 Sep (Interesting Engineering), Ynetnews about 4 Oct, recirculated by Slashdot and ScienceAlert on 13 Oct — a three-week-old release still circulating. (source: https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/bjwnvogsgl ; https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/super-ice-created-rivals-concrete-in-strength)
- **Recycled: the "most extreme rogue wave on record" is a 2020 wave and a 2022 paper** — ScienceAlert's trending list this week marks the Ucluelet rogue-wave story as posted "1 day ago"; the article page fetched is dated 8 Jan 2025 and itself states "An earlier version of this article was published in February 2022". The wave struck 17 Nov 2020 off Ucluelet, Vancouver Island, measured 17.6 m and was nearly three times the height of surrounding waves (Johannes Gemmrich and Leah Cicon, University of Victoria, Scientific Reports, 2022). No 2026 study displacing it was found. Labelled recycled. (source: https://www.sciencealert.com/a-gigantic-wave-in-the-pacific-ocean-was-the-most-extreme-rogue-wave-on-record)

- **OpenAI dismisses three safety researchers; the two sides dispute why** — OpenAI confirmed (RTTNews, 2 Oct) it had fired three researchers after an internal investigation found they handled sensitive information outside established procedures; in an X post on 9 Oct it called the conduct a "significant breach of trust" and said the dismissals were "not retaliation" for raising safety concerns (as quoted by Online Tech Tips, 10 Oct, citing AFP, The Verge and AP via Yahoo). The three — Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni — wrote an open letter around 8 Oct to OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee saying the firings are "chilling the open culture OpenAI has prized in the past"; Balesni posted that they were fired for "prioritizing safety over the near-term interest of OpenAI as a corporation". Korbak says he was told his dismissal related to how he communicated with METR, the independent evaluator OpenAI engaged after its summer agent-sandbox incident. OpenAI has not said which information or which policy was involved. Happened early Oct; published 2–10 Oct. (source: https://www.rttnews.com/3696251/openai-fires-three-researchers-over-mishandling-of-sensitive-information.aspx?type=ts ; https://www.online-tech-tips.com/?p=111208)
- **NVIDIA releases Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and Bolt open-weight models** — On 15 Oct NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (120 billion total parameters, 12 billion active; a vision-language model for agents working over documents, charts and web pages) and Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B (native 2-bit quantisation to fit in 24 GB), with a smaller Bolt E4B due 23 Oct. NVIDIA's own claims as reported: task completion five times faster than the nearest competitor in class, accuracy approaching its largest reasoning model with about one-fifth the parameters, training on about 26 trillion tokens. No independent evaluation cited; licence not named in the coverage seen. Happened 15 Oct; published 15 Oct. (source: https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/nvidia-nemotron-35-super-vl-5x-faster)
