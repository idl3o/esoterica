# /head-canon — Canonise the Unwritten

You are running **head-canonisation**: the inverse of a fiction bridge. A bridge *extracts* the consciousness technology already in a canon. Head-canon *extends* the canon into territory the story never wrote — an unwritten scene — and reads the insight off what you are **forced to invent** to make the scene honest.

The pun is load-bearing. *Canonisation* is also how a saint is made. You are putting the character's stance on trial: does the technology the bridge found actually *sanctify* under the pressure of unwritten time — is it livable — or did it only work because the story ended before it could be tested?

The user will specify (or you should choose): **which bridge?** Head-canon needs a world with *continuable life*. Character/narrative bridges qualify. Concept-bridges (Wittgenstein, the readymade, the realist novel, the vignette-as-form) do not — they have no one to wake up later. If the target has no continuable life, say so and stop.

---

## READ THIS FIRST: the formula to refuse

A wave of six pilot vignettes, written by independent authors who could not see each other, collapsed into a **single poem written six times**. Every one of them:

- displaced to the **deep future / old age**;
- narrated from a **younger mortal witness** watching a **hollowed-out immortal** protagonist;
- hung the weight on **one inherited ordinary object**;
- and closed on a **long, em-dashed, periodic final sentence** that left the protagonist's interior **undecidable** — *is it the real thing, or its hollow twin? no one can tell.*

That is the house attractor. It is the corpus's single favourite gesture (the beautiful-undecidable-held-open), and it will try to write your vignette *for* you. Six strangers proves it is a shared prior, not a discovery. **Your one job is to not write that poem again.** Concretely:

- **Vary at least two axes** away from the formula: the displacement, the POV, whether anything happens, the register, the shape of the ending. If you find yourself writing a mortal grandchild watching an ancient hero over a family heirloom, stop and change two things.
- **Do not end on the undecidable.** No "she could not tell whether X or its hollow twin." No trailing em-dash spiral into the unanswerable. End on an *event*, an *image*, a *line of dialogue*, a *decision* — something with edges.
- **Let something happen.** The pilot vignettes were static tableaux: a watcher observing an unchanging figure. Put a small plot in the scene — a task, a visit, an argument, a mishap, a choice — that begins and *changes* and can even resolve at its own small scale, while the bridge's deep question stays open in the background.
- **Let the source's native tone lead.** If the canon is funny, be funny. If it is warm, be warm. If it is pulpy or absurd or adventurous, be that. "The excluded register" (below) does not mean "always go sad" — for half the bridges in this repo, the register the source refuses is *lightness*, not gravity.

## The five principles (these are the method — hold them, don't recite them)

1. **Extend, don't continue.** Do not write "what happens next." A plot is a machine for reaching resolution; continuing it inherits that engine. Jump *off* the timeline so the characters stop being *driven* and simply have to *exist*. A slice reveals what a plot would rush past.

2. **Write the register the canon excludes — which is not always the sad one.** A negative-space move aimed at *tone*. If the source is loud, the person may be in the quiet; if the source is grim, the person may be in the joke; if the source is relentlessly hopeful, the person may be in the boredom. Find the tone *this* source refuses, and it will not be melancholy every time. Melancholy is the default you are here to beat.

3. **Read insight off forced invention.** Do NOT decide the theme and illustrate it. Write the specific scene honestly, and let the details the scene *demands* tell you what is true. The insight you did not have going in is the only one worth keeping.

4. **Displace to audit — and don't reach for deep-time by reflex.** Choose the displacement that makes *this* source's philosophy live past its ending. Deep-time is ONE option and the overused one. Prefer, when they fit better: an ordinary day mid-story, a defeat, a retirement, a role-reversal, a small domestic event, the morning after a win, a minor character's POV on a Tuesday. Pick the sharpest stress-test, not the epic one.

5. **The tooth is a constraint, not the subject.** The bridge holds a tension open (read it — usually near the end). Honour it by **not falsely settling it** — but do NOT make the scene a meditation *on* it. Hold it *ambient*, in the background, while the scene is about ordinary life. The deep question stays open because you **leave it alone**, not because you end the vignette pointing at it. A scene can resolve its own small plot completely and still leave the bridge's big question exactly where it found it.

## The honest caution

Head-canon is the **easiest instrument in the repository to lie with.** A bridge is disciplined by an existing text — you can be *wrong* about what is on the page. Head-canon has no page to be wrong against; nothing forbids you; you are free to write the world you want. That makes it the most seductive form of the convergence-engine there is.

The proof the pilot handed us: banning the *beauty* default just promoted the *undecidable-melancholy* default. Forbidding one house-ending promotes the runner-up. So do not merely avoid a tone — **make something specific to this world happen**, and the attractor loses its grip. The test that the method reads *the world* and not the *house style*: point it at six worlds and get six genuinely different *moves*, not one move in six costumes.

## Step 1: Probe

Read the target bridge in `fiction-bridges/[slug].md` in full. From it, name — in your own head before you write a line:

- **The tooth** — the unresolved tension the bridge holds open. You are bound *not to settle* it. You are NOT obliged to be *about* it.
- **The native tone the source refuses** — and check honestly whether that is gravity or *lightness*.
- **The displacement** — the sharpest stress-test, not the most epic. Deep-time only if it genuinely beats the alternatives for this source.
- **What happens** — the small plot. A scene, not a tableau. Something that begins, turns, and lands.
- **The aesthetic verdict you expect** — predicted honestly, and if it's "melancholy," push back on yourself once before accepting it.

## Step 2: Write the vignette

Write `vignettes/[slug].md`. This is **literature, not analysis** — a crafted scene with something happening in it.

- **~800–1,500 words.** Restraint and compression, not length. This is the opposite of the bridge's "go long."
- **Show displacement through small strangenesses**, never exposition.
- **Dialogue can carry the scene.** It does not have to be oblique-and-withholding; people can be funny, blunt, ordinary.
- **Let one action or object anchor it** — but not, by reflex, an inherited heirloom passed down a family line.
- **End with edges.** An event, an image, a decision, a spoken line. Not a spiral into the unanswerable.
- **Keep the tooth open by leaving it alone** — ambient, not climactic.

Close with an italic provenance footer: `Head-canon of [[the-source-bridge-slug]] — <the displacement used> · <the register found>.`

## Step 3: Report (for the diversity-gate)

Return — concisely:
- the **displacement**, the **POV**, and the **register/aesthetic verdict** you landed on (be honest if it drifted);
- **what happens** in the scene (the small plot);
- the **tooth** you kept, and confirm you held it *ambient* rather than made it the subject/ending;
- the **forced invention** — the detail the scene demanded that carried the insight;
- **which two-plus axes** you deliberately varied away from the formula;
- word count.

---

## Principles

- **The scene knows more than the plan.** If you knew the insight before you wrote, you illustrated; you did not head-canon.
- **Something happens.** A slice-of-life is still a *life* — it moves. A static meditation on an unchanging figure is the formula; refuse it.
- **Different worlds, different verdicts.** Your one contribution to the wave's honesty is to write *this* world's truth in *this* world's register — and to make certain it is not the poem the last five agents already wrote.
