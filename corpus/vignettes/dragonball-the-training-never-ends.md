# The Step That Goes Down

The chamber died at four hundred and fifty.

Not gracefully. There was a cough somewhere in the compressor, a smell like a scorched senzu, and then the four hundred and fifty gravities of crushing weight that Vegeta had spent the whole morning learning to breathe under simply *left* him. He staggered. The Prince of All Saiyans staggered — because his body, braced against a load that would flatten a mountain, suddenly found nothing to push against and nearly put him through the wall out of sheer readiness.

He stood in the ringing quiet. The chamber's hum was the sound he'd fallen asleep to for fifteen years. Its absence had a texture. He put a hand on the console. Dead panel. He tried the reboot. Dead panel. He tried the reboot again, harder, which he knew was stupid and did anyway, because *harder* was the only tool the morning had given him.

The intercom crackled. Bulma, from somewhere with an echo. "Before you break it worse — the compressor coil's shot, I don't have the part, supplier says three days." A pause that already knew what he was going to say. "*Three days*, Vegeta. And you've got Bulla. I told you this morning. Capsule board till six."

"I heard you this morning."

"You didn't hear me this morning."

He hadn't heard her this morning.

Click.

---

Bulla was eleven months old and, at present, unhappy about it.

He found her in the playroom going a magnificent, building red, fists shaking, the specific cry that meant the world had failed her in some way she could not itemize. Vegeta crouched at combat distance and read her the way he read everything: for the tell. Fighters had tells. The wind-up before the strike, the hitch before the feint. If he could find the source of the attack he could counter it.

He found nothing. No injury. No hunger — she'd eaten. Dry, warm, held. Every diagnostic clean, and still the alarm screamed on.

"State your grievance," he told his daughter.

His daughter escalated.

*Fine.* He knew escalation. He had made a religion of it. If the pressure was rising, you rose to meet the pressure — that was the whole science, the thing Beerus had beaten into him: fill your mind with one intention and let it become total. He picked her up. He planted his feet. He met the sound the way he had learned to meet a blow that could not be dodged — stopped bracing against it and let it *land*, let it pass into him, waiting for the old alchemy where impact becomes fuel and the receiving of damage makes you larger than the thing that damaged you.

He waited to get stronger.

He did not get stronger. He got a headache, and a wetter shoulder, and a child who had somehow found a *further* gear, and the dawning, insulting recognition that Ultra Ego does not work on a baby, because a baby is not attacking you. There was no blow to convert. There was no opponent. The engine that ran on being struck had nothing to run on, and it idled, and it was useless, and beneath the noise Vegeta felt the small cold thing he felt perhaps twice a decade, which was: *I do not know how to do this, and there is no one stronger I can go and beat until I do.*

He walked her. He bounced her the way he'd seen Bulma do it, which felt like a technique he was performing badly. He tried talking, low and even, and his even voice was a war-briefing voice, and she cried through it. He carried her to the window and pointed at nothing, at the sky, and remembered only her mother could ride the somersault cloud and he could not, which was its own small humiliation on a day already full of them.

Two hours. No gravity chamber humming him toward the next threshold. No tournament on the calendar. Beerus asleep for who knew how long. The whole vertical ladder of his life — the always-one-more-rung of it, the thing that made a day mean something — folded flat into this: a room, a Tuesday, a crying that would not resolve into a fight he could win.

He stopped in the middle of the floor.

And because he had exhausted every direction that went *up*, he looked, for the first time in his life, at the only direction left.

He powered down.

Not the theatrical way, not the flare-and-settle he did before a spar. He let his ki *go*. He let it sink past the resting hum a Saiyan never fully silences, past the low idle he kept even in sleep, down and down until a human standing in the doorway would not have felt a warrior in the room at all — would have felt nothing, an empty space, a man. He made himself less. He made himself *nothing*, the way the chamber was nothing now: no pressure, no load, no readiness leaning on the air.

Bulla stopped.

Not all at once. A hitch. A shuddering intake. Then her small hand, which had been a fist, opened flat against his collarbone, and she looked up at him with the wet, startled clarity of someone who had been afraid of something that was suddenly not there.

He understood it then, standing in the dead-quiet room, and the understanding had teeth. It had been *him*. All this time, the pressure she'd been crying against. He walked through every room of his life at a low battle-idle, ki banked like a stormfront, a being permanently three seconds from a fight the universe kept promising him — and to an infant that idle was *weather*. She had been trying, for two hours, to tell him to stand down, and he had answered by turning the storm up.

There was no power level for what she'd needed. It was not on the scale. It was *off the bottom* of the scale. The whole map of his becoming had one direction — more, higher, past the ceiling, find someone stronger — and here was a step it did not have, a step that went *down*, into less than himself, and it turned out that was the one she'd been waiting on the whole time.

She was asleep before he'd finished the thought. Fast, complete, the way only the very small go under. Fist unclenched. Weight settling into full trust against a chest that had, ten minutes ago, been frightening her.

He did not move. Moving was a technique and he could not, at that moment, afford one. He stood in the ruined afternoon holding the one being in twelve universes he had no strategy for, keeping his ki at a level he'd never once trained toward, which was: barely there. Barely there was hard. Barely there took everything. His arms began, distantly, to ache — an ache with no name, since it came entirely from holding still.

He heard the capsule car in the drive at five past six. Bulma's steps, the door, her voice already mid-sentence — "so the coil is *definitely* not coming till—" and then it cut out.

She stood in the playroom doorway with her keys still in her hand and looked at the two of them: the Prince of All Saiyans planted dead center of the floor like he was holding a summit against a siege, radiating, for once in his life, absolutely nothing.

"The chamber's still down," Vegeta said, very quietly, not turning his head.

"I know." Bulma set the keys on the shelf without a sound.

"I require you to not fix it until she wakes."

Bulma looked at him a moment longer. Then she crossed the room and stood beside him, close, her shoulder against his arm, and she did not say a single one of the things he could see her deciding not to say.

"Three days," she said instead. "Think you'll survive it."

"No," said Vegeta, and meant it, and did not move, and the room stayed quiet, and the training — the real training, the kind with no rung above it and no one stronger to go and find — went on.
