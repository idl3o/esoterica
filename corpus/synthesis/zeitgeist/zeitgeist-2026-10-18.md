## CORRESPONDENCE
*The same pattern at every scale.*

The week's pattern is **the unsigned effect**: the consequence arrives, and the name of its author is withheld, disputed or supplied by somebody else. At the personal scale a viewer argues whether Rumpelstiltskin is from 1987, and a persona's audience halves at the moment its author is named. The counter-cases are where the signing was done: Singapore's notices; the physics prize, given for tracing high-energy neutrinos to an astrophysical origin after years of catching them under the ice; the builders' most-upvoted post of the week, whose title is the origin of a curve.

Three cautions. The idea is old: international relations calls it the attribution problem, statistics calls it causal inference, and the courts have standing and burden of proof for it. The pattern is kin to the last reading's missing threshold; the difference is that a threshold is a number and a signature is a name, and this week the numbers mostly arrived while the names did not. And a reading that goes looking for unsigned effects will find them, because most effects are. It earns its place only by a sorting the reader can check: which effects got their author named this week by a party with standing, and which were named by implication, by hashtag, by prediction market, or not at all. Singapore signed, and so did the physicists. The strait, the dismissals, the truce and the trending lists did not.
