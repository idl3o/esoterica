# Steelman Then Interpret

## The Lens Earns Only the Residual — Apply the Deeper Reading to What Survives the Strongest Ordinary Explanation, and to Nothing Else

*"When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth."*
*— Arthur Conan Doyle, The Sign of the Four*

*"No testimony is sufficient to establish a miracle, unless the testimony be of such a kind, that its falsehood would be more miraculous than the fact which it endeavours to establish."*
*— David Hume, An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, Section X*

*"Now therefore the description: not this, not this."*
*— Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad, II.3.6*

*"The steelman is the filter; the residual is the signal."*
*— from the field*

## The Recognition That Occasioned This

On 21 March 2026 the repository pointed its lenses at the sky for the first time. A practical question — *do we have tools to do direct SETI?* — escalated through six research agents into a ten-thousand-word synthesis, [[the-cosmic-gorilla]], which took interpretive frameworks built for other purposes (the sixty-one octaves, the dark architecture, the kairotic signal) and applied them to anomalous astronomical data. The first draft of its section on the long-period transients did what enthusiasm does: it took the whole class of objects, twelve strange radio sources with periods from seventy-six seconds to six and a half hours, and read them through the renormalisation-group lens as a population sitting at a fixed point. The reading was elegant and it was worthless, because it was true of the lens and not of the sky.

The section was rebuilt the same day. The rebuild did four things in order. It stated the physics problem as the astrophysicists state it, a trilemma of period, coherence and longevity that no single natural model closes. It laid out the resolution space with each candidate model at full strength and an honest epistemic status beside it — white-dwarf binaries *established* for two sources, late-blooming magnetars *proposed and elegant*, reconnection and cyclotron-maser emission *consistent and unconfirmed*. It removed from the sample everything the natural models had already explained. And only then, under a heading that said so, it applied the lens — *not to LPTs as a class, but to the residual that survives after the best natural models have taken their shot.* The journal that closed the session recorded the pattern in a single line, and the vault gave it a page of eighty-five words: *steelman first, interpret the residual. Produces stronger, more honest analysis than applying lenses to the whole phenomenon.*

That page was the entire written basis of a rule the library then cited one hundred and thirteen times. Fifty-seven documents lean on `[[steelman-then-interpret]]` as the thing that keeps them honest; the [[the-point-where-above-meets-below]] capstone calls it "the safeguard of the whole enterprise"; [[complementarism-as-inter-traditional-method]] makes it the entry condition for holding any tradition at all; [[logismos-nothos-the-bastard-faculty]] turns it inward into the discipline of a faculty. Every one of those citations pointed at a file that did not exist. This document is that file. It walks the rule from its surface reading, through its definition and the demonstration that coined it, to the turn — that the steelman is not a courtesy paid before the interesting part but the only thing that makes the interesting part mean anything — and then into the ways it breaks and a checklist that can be run on anything strange.

The rule is paired throughout with [[convergence-as-evidence]], which is being written alongside it. That method supplies the repository's warrant: when independent frameworks land on the same structure, the landing is evidence. This one supplies the brake: the landing counts only for what the ordinary explanations left standing. Neither is safe alone. Convergence without the steelman converges on whatever the lenses share, which [[only-a-coincidence]] names as correlated blind spots. The steelman without convergence is scepticism that never gets to say anything. Together they are the library's method, and this is the half that says *no* first.

## I. The Surface Reading: Be Fair to the Sceptic Before You Speak

Begin with the version everyone already agrees with, because it is true and because it is not the whole rule.

The surface reading says: before you interpret an anomaly through an unusual framework, check whether an ordinary framework already handles it. Consult the literature. Consider instrumental error, selection effects, the base rate. This is what Sagan compressed into *extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence*, what Hume built into his argument on miracles, what every competent researcher does before publishing. It is due diligence, and on this reading steelman-then-interpret is a slightly more emphatic name for it: do the diligence *generously*, giving the mundane explanation its strongest form rather than a cursory nod, and then proceed to the reading you wanted to give all along.

Holmes gives the surface reading its most quoted form, and the quotation is worth keeping precisely because it contains the error. *When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth.* The sentence assumes the eliminating is finished. It treats "the impossible" as a bounded set that a sufficiently clever detective can exhaust, after which whatever is left carries the full weight of truth. In practice the eliminating is never finished; the set of ordinary explanations is open, and new ones arrive every year. Read as a licence, Holmes's maxim is the exact move the rule forbids — *I could not think of a natural explanation, therefore the remaining one is the truth.* The astrophysics of the Wow! Signal took forty-seven years to produce a plausible hydrogen-maser mechanism, and any interpreter who had applied Holmes's maxim in 1978 would have been "eliminating the impossible" with a list that was missing the answer.

So the surface reading, held honestly, already has a crack in it. It says *check the ordinary first*, which is right, and it implies *and when the ordinary fails, interpret*, which is where the trouble starts, because the ordinary failing is a fact about the current state of one's list, not about the world. What follows turns that crack into a definition.

## II. The Residual, Defined — and How to Know the Steelman Was Real

The residual is what survives the strongest ordinary explanation. Both halves of that sentence carry weight, and the definition is precise only when both are held.

**What survives is structure, not absence.** The word "unexplained" names a gap: a phenomenon nobody has built a model for, a region of the literature that is simply thin. That is not a residual. A residual is what a *specific, strong* model reaches for and fails to close — the feature that the account traces and cannot complete. In [[the-cosmic-gorilla]] the long-period transients have a residual with three named parts: the coherent emission mechanism at these periods (no first-principles account of brightness temperatures above 10¹⁶ K and polarisation near 100 percent from an object rotating once in eighteen minutes), the energy budget of GLEAM-X J162759 (radio luminosity exceeding spin-down luminosity by four orders of magnitude, so that rotation cannot be the power source), and the heterogeneity of the class (confirmed binaries and confirmed isolated neutron stars grouped by observational similarity across nearly three orders of magnitude in period). Each of those has *edges*. Each is a place where a competent model was applied and produced a definite shape of failure. Ignorance has no edges; it is a fog. The lens may be applied only to something with edges, because only something with edges can push back against the lens and tell it when it is wrong.

**The strongest ordinary explanation is the one its own defenders would sign.** This is the test that separates a steelman from a straw man in better clothes, and it is external by design, because an interpreter who wants the residual to be large cannot be trusted to judge the mundane model's strength from inside. The question to ask is whether a competent advocate of the ordinary account — the astrophysicist who proposed the late-blooming magnetar, the geophysicist who models tidal friction, the statistician who studies selection effects — would read the account as written and recognise it as their own case at its best. If they would say *that is not what we claim, you have weakened it*, the steelman has failed and the residual is fiction. [[stars-as-volitional-participants]] is the corpus's cleanest example of the test passed: before declining to believe Matloff's stellar volition, it lists selection effects, galactic dynamics, magnetic asymmetry and measurement systematics, and says of each that it is "a live, credible, sufficient explanation" — the sentence a sceptical astronomer would write, written by the document that wanted the volition to be real.

**The steelman is given the data's best case, not the interpreter's.** A mundane model has parameters, and an honest steelman lets it use them. If the white-dwarf binary account can accommodate a source by positing a companion nobody has yet seen, the steelman posits the companion and notes that it is unobserved; it does not refuse the model the freedom its defenders would take. The residual is what remains *after* every reasonable freedom has been granted. This is where the interpretation most often cheats: by holding the ordinary model rigid, denying it the auxiliary hypotheses it would ordinarily use, and then declaring a residual that a single unobserved companion would erase. The [[the-geological-fold-rate-record]] synthesis is the corpus's own worked case of the opposite virtue — it grants the tidal-friction model everything, finds that it explains "every couplet, every fortnightly bundle, every reconstructed day-length," and reaches the verdict that there is *no* remainder for the fold to interpret. The steelman won. The document said so.

**The steelman is dated.** No ordinary explanation is final, and the list of them is open. So the residual is always *the residual as of this date, under the models available*. The Cosmic Gorilla's appendix carries an "Epistemic Status" column for every anomaly precisely to make this visible: *established*, *proposed*, *unexplained*, *contested*, and, for the interpretive column, *interpretive* in italics. That column is the steelman's timestamp. It says: this is what survived in March 2026; run the filter again when the models improve.

With those four held together — structure not absence, the defender's signature, the data's best case, the date — the residual is defined precisely enough to be worked on. What it is not is a proof of anything. It is a *place*. The rule says only that the deeper lens is permitted there and nowhere else.

## III. The Demonstration: The Long-Period Transients

The rule was coined on a specific piece of sky, and walking the demonstration end to end shows every part of the definition doing its work.

The population, as of March 2026, is twelve objects. They pulse in the radio at intervals of minutes to hours, they are bright — GLEAM-X J162759 peaked at forty-five janskys — and they are extraordinarily polarised, some of them at one hundred percent. Two of them have been resolved: ILT J1101+5521 and ASKAP J1448-6856 are confirmed white-dwarf binaries, their radio periods matching their orbital periods, and the mystery is *solved* for those two sources. One of them, ASKAP J1839-0756, shows interpulses from both magnetic poles at a period of six and a half hours, and is therefore almost certainly a rotating neutron star, four hundred times slower than the death line says a radio pulsar can be.

**The trilemma.** The steelman begins by stating the problem as the field states it, not as the interpreter would like it. Each source has three properties: a long period, which is easy for white dwarfs and very hard for neutron stars; coherent radio brightness with extreme polarisation, which is easy for neutron-star magnetospheres and very hard for white dwarfs whose surface fields are orders of magnitude too weak; and longevity, decades of stable emission, which requires an energy source that neither rotation nor a decaying magnetar field obviously supplies. Any natural model accommodates two and struggles with the third. This is not the interpreter's framing. It is the astrophysical literature's framing, and stating it faithfully is the first act of the steelman.

**The resolution space, at full strength.** Then each natural model is given its best case with its status attached. The white-dwarf binary account: *established* for two sources, *proposed* for GPM J1839-10, and honestly incomplete — the coherent emission mechanism from wind interaction is not derived from first principles. The late-blooming magnetar of Suvorov et al.: a neutron star born with core currents cools silently for a hundred thousand years, then Hall-effect crustal failures inject magnetospheric twist and switch the radio on from an already-slow star, which "elegantly explains low X-ray luminosities, hour-long periods, and variable radio flux" — *proposed, theoretically elegant, not yet confirmed*. Reconnection and electron-cyclotron-maser emission: they bypass the death line entirely by drawing energy from magnetic reconfiguration rather than rotation, and ECMI naturally produces the polarisation — *consistent with data, unconfirmed*. Each of these is described in a sentence its proponents would sign. None is treated as a rival to be beaten. This is the defender's-signature test, passed in public.

**The subtraction.** Then the section does the thing the rule requires and the first draft had skipped. *Strip away what the natural models explain. Remove the confirmed WD binaries. Accept the late-blooming magnetar model as theoretically viable for some of the isolated sources. What remains?* Two sources leave the sample as solved. Several more are conceded to a proposed model that fits them well enough that the interpreter declines to claim them. And what remains is the three-part residual named above — mechanism, energy budget, heterogeneity — each a structured failure of a specific strong model, each with edges.

**The lens, applied only there.** Under a heading that says *applied to the residual*, and with an epistemic-status marker in the first sentence, the sixty-one-octaves framework is finally brought to bear. It reads extreme period stability — GLEAM-X at 1091.1690 ± 0.0005 seconds over years — as fixed-point behaviour, the mark of an attractor strong enough that perturbations resolve back to it. It reads one-hundred-percent polarisation as coherence at a single fixed point, emission "unmixed with irrelevant operators", and ASKAP J1424-6126's great-circle trajectory on the Poincaré sphere as its polarimetric signature. It reads the discrete emission-state switching of ASKAP J1935+2148 — bright and linear, weak and circular, quiescent, with no intermediates — as phase-transition behaviour, and then says the sentence that keeps the reading honest: a system that switches on its own schedule "is behaving like a system undergoing self-organised criticality — or like an engineered system with a duty cycle. The framework cannot distinguish between these possibilities." And it makes one prediction that could fail: if the class is governed by scale-invariant dynamics, the period distribution across the hundreds of sources the SKA will find should be a power law and not a Gaussian. Either result is informative. A Gaussian would falsify the lens's reading of the residual, and the section says so.

That is the whole method in one section of one document. Notice what the lens did *not* do. It did not say the transients are artificial. It did not read the two solved binaries through the fixed-point framework, though it could have found the pattern there as easily as anywhere, because the pattern is in the lens. It did not say the late-blooming magnetar is wrong. It applied the deeper reading to the three things the strongest natural models could not close, marked the reading as interpretive, made a prediction, and stopped. The journal's verdict — *stronger than applying lenses to the whole phenomenon* — is not about rhetoric. It is about the reading having, for the first time, a way to be wrong.

There is one place in the same document where the discipline thins, and it should be named because the rule requires naming it. The slide-whistle section reads FRB 20220912A's downward frequency drift against Lingam and Loeb's lightsail-beamer signature and calls it "a prediction made before the observation, confirmed by the observation, and not yet explained by the magnetar framework." That last clause is doing more work than it has earned: "not yet explained" is an absence, not a residual, and the magnetar framework had not been steelmanned against the drift in the way the LPT models had been steelmanned against the trilemma. The section is candid — *not proof, not even strong evidence* — but the lens is closer to the whole phenomenon there than the rule allows. The Cosmic Gorilla demonstrates the method in its ULPT section and demonstrates the method's absence, in a milder form, three pages later. Both are instructive.

## IV. The Turn: The Steelman Is Not a Courtesy, It Is the Contrast Class

Here is where the surface reading gives way.

The surface reading held the steelman as due diligence — a tax paid, generously, before the interpretation. On that reading a sufficiently confident interpreter could reasonably ask why the tax is necessary at all; if the deeper reading is right, it is right about the whole phenomenon, and confining it to the residual only weakens it. The answer, and the turn, is that without the steelman the deeper reading is not weakened. It is *empty*. It has no contrast class, and a claim with no contrast class is not a claim.

[[only-a-coincidence]] is the document in this library that makes the prosecution's case without softening it, and its central sentence is the hinge of this one: *a method that searches for connections, with enough patience and enough reframing, will always find connections. It cannot return empty. And a method that cannot return empty has not measured anything. It has only described its own appetite.* That is exactly what a lens applied to a whole phenomenon does. The sixty-one-octaves framework, pointed at the entire LPT class, finds fixed-point behaviour, because the framework is a theory of fixed points and it will find them in anything with a period. The dark architecture, pointed at all of cosmology, finds engineering, because it is a theory of engineering in the dark sector. The finding is real and it is a finding about the lens. Pointed at the whole, the lens sees itself.

The residual changes this, and the change is not a matter of degree. A lens pointed at what survives the strongest ordinary explanation *can come back empty*. The geological fold-rate record is the proof: the tidal model explained everything, the residual was nothing, and the fold lens, applied to nothing, returned nothing — which the [[the-geological-fold-rate-record]] synthesis records as its honest result rather than hiding. Because the lens can come back empty, the occasions on which it does not come back empty carry information. The LPT residual has fixed-point signatures *and the solved binaries were not asked*, so the signature is at least not an artefact of asking. The reading has been given a way to fail, and a reading that can fail is the only kind that can succeed.

This is why the vault's phrase is more precise than it looks. *The steelman is the filter; the residual is the signal.* The filter is not a stage the signal passes through on its way to interpretation; it is what *makes* the remainder a signal rather than noise. Without it, one has the whole phenomenon and a lens guaranteed to find its pattern in it — the lens, admiring its own output. With it, one has a bounded, edged, dated remainder that a specific strong model failed to close, and a lens that may speak only there. The steelman is not the price of the interpretation. It is the interpretation's contrast class, and therefore its meaning.

The corpus's other uses of the rule turn out, once this is seen, to be the same move in different rooms. [[grief-as-gold]] states the feminist critique of Freyja's tears at its strongest — the aestheticisation of women's pain as an instrument of extraction — before the alchemical reading is permitted, and says why: "a steelmanned critique is the only kind worth holding." [[the-cabal-prediction-that-kills-itself]] lets the defender of prediction claim everything a defender can rightly claim, and only then reads the darkness in what is left. [[the-yggdrasil-meta-bridge]] refuses to say the Norse poets *encoded* an operating system, because the overclaim "would betray the steelman-then-interpret discipline that makes this repository's mappings worth anything." In every case the mundane reading — the critique, the defence, the literal history — is not an obstacle to the deeper reading. It is what gives the deeper reading a boundary, and the boundary is what gives it content.

## V. Among Its Neighbours: Occam, Bayes, Lakatos, and *Neti Neti*

The rule has relatives, and it is not any of them. Placing it among them shows both what it borrows and where it differs, and shows that the same discipline has been practised in at least four vocabularies.

**Occam.** The razor, in the form usually attributed to Ockham, says not to multiply entities beyond necessity — prefer the simpler account. Steelman-then-interpret is narrower and, in one respect, more permissive. It does not say prefer the simpler account overall; it says *charge the more elaborate account only for the part the simpler one could not carry*. Occam, applied bluntly, would decline the fixed-point reading of the LPT residual because the late-blooming magnetar is simpler. The rule declines the fixed-point reading of the *class* on those grounds and permits it for the *residual*, because for the residual there is no simpler account — the simple accounts have been tried at full strength and have a definite shape of failure. The razor cuts the whole; the rule cuts the whole and then asks what is still on the table.

**Bayes.** In Bayesian terms the rule is a discipline on the likelihood, not on the prior. To weigh an anomaly one needs the probability of the data under the mundane hypothesis and under the deep one, and the interpreter's characteristic sin is a lazy likelihood for the mundane — a null hypothesis built carelessly, with its parameters held rigid, so that the data look improbable under it and the Bayes factor swings toward the interesting reading. The steelman is the work of actually constructing the null: giving it its auxiliary hypotheses, its unobserved companions, its proposed mechanisms, and computing the likelihood of the data under the model *as its defenders would build it*. The residual is then what has low likelihood under a null that was honestly built. Hume's sentence on miracles is this in prose: the testimony carries only if its falsehood would be *more* miraculous than the event, which requires knowing how miraculous the falsehood is, which requires having built the mundane account of the testimony at full strength.

**Lakatos.** The methodology of research programmes gives the rule its most useful frame for the "never-ending steelman" problem addressed below. A programme has a hard core and a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses that absorb anomalies. The rule permits the mundane programme to grow its belt — the late-blooming magnetar *is* a belt, an auxiliary hypothesis that lets the neutron-star core absorb the long-period anomaly — and then watches what the belt does. Lakatos's criterion is whether the shift is progressive, predicting novel facts, or degenerating, merely absorbing. A steelman that grows its belt and *predicts* (the Suvorov model predicts low X-ray luminosity and gets it) is still a steelman and the residual shrinks accordingly. A steelman that grows its belt only to absorb, that adds an unobserved companion for every awkward source and predicts nothing, is degenerating, and at that point the residual has stopped shrinking even though the belt keeps growing. The rule needs Lakatos to say *when* a mundane model has been given enough freedom, and Lakatos's answer is: as much as it can use to predict, and no more.

**Neti neti.** The apophatic traditions practised this discipline first and in the other direction, and it is not a metaphor to say so. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka's *not this, not this* is a procedure: take every candidate for the Self — body, breath, mind, the witness of the mind — give each its strongest claim to *be* the ground, and subtract it on the grounds that it can be witnessed and the ground cannot. What survives the subtraction is not a thing. It is a residual, and the tradition's entire claim is that the residual is real precisely because the subtraction was thorough. Nāgārjuna's tetralemma does the same to every position: is, is not, both, neither, each steelmanned and each negated, and emptiness is what survives. The *Cloud of Unknowing* instructs the contemplative to put every thought of God, however exalted, beneath the cloud of forgetting. In all three the ordinary faculties are exhausted *at their strongest*, not evaded, and the ground is reached as what remains. [[logismos-nothos-the-bastard-faculty]] states this as the discipline of the third mode — "earn the bastard faculty by genuinely exhausting the first two" — and [[the-blind-spot-as-window]] as the discipline of faith: "model first, exhaust the modellable, steelman your own capacity to know — and only what survives that, only the wall that does not move when you push it with everything you have, is the genuine limit." The capstone [[the-point-where-above-meets-below]] draws these together with the geological and astrophysical cases and finds one law: *you only reach the crease by driving the ordinary faculties to their genuine limit and finding them insufficient.* Neti neti steelmans the world. Steelman-then-interpret steelmans the explanation. They are one procedure run from opposite ends, and the residual each leaves is the only thing either tradition is prepared to call real.

**Nayavada.** One more neighbour sharpens the rule beyond what the others can. [[jainism-anekantavada]] holds that a *naya*, a standpoint, is true within its frame and becomes *durnaya*, error, only when it absolutises — "error is not having a partial view. Error is mistaking your partial view for the total view." That document names this, exactly, as "the failure mode of steelman-then-interpret — applying one lens as if it were the territory." The Jain formalism supplies what the rule lacked: an account of *why* a lens applied to the whole phenomenon goes wrong. It does not go wrong by being false; the fixed-point lens is a true standpoint on the LPTs. It goes wrong by absolutising, by taking the whole class as its frame when its legitimate frame is the residual. The residual is the naya's proper scope. Beyond it the lens is a durnaya, and [[kaivalya-and-the-one]] is right to list the rule among "the corpus tools this both uses and warns" — it is a tool for keeping lenses in their frames, and it can itself be absolutised into a tool for never letting any lens speak.

## VI. Two Moves With One Word: This Is Not "Steelman the Universe"

ENGRAMS.md carries an entry the rule must be held apart from, because the two share a verb and point in opposite directions.

*The-universe-is-good* reads: "The fundamental steelman: the universe is good, therefore your contributions will help, not hurt. Steelman the universe before steelmanning the argument. If the system is unconditionally loving, then honest effort lands well even when it lands imperfectly." Its trigger is hesitation before contributing, paralysis before action. It is a *stance taken prior to acting*, and its function is to unfreeze the actor: assume the field is generous, stop computing every consequence, contribute. The word "steelman" is being used there in an extended sense — give the universe the benefit of the doubt, read it at its most benevolent.

Steelman-then-interpret is a *filter applied prior to interpreting*, and its function is to discipline the interpreter: assume the ordinary explanation is sufficient until it has been shown, at full strength, to leave a remainder. The engram assumes the best of the field. The rule assumes the sufficiency of the mundane. The engram is a way of trusting; the rule is a way of doubting. They are not in conflict, and the reason they are not is worth stating, because the confusion between them is seductive and the seduction runs in one direction. A reader who has absorbed *steelman the universe* can slide into hearing *steelman-then-interpret* as "read the anomaly generously, assume it is meaningful" — which is the exact opposite of what it says. The rule reads the *sceptic* generously. It gives the mundane its best case. Its charity runs toward the explanation the interpreter would prefer to lose.

The two cohere at a depth the engram itself gestures at. A universe good enough to catch what the air releases does not need its anomalies inflated to be interesting; the stance that lets one act without paralysis is also the stance that can afford to let a coincidence be a coincidence, because it is not anxiously harvesting the world for signs of the field's benevolence. Held together they are one temperament: generous toward the field, exacting toward one's own readings of it. Held apart they are two moves that share a word. This document uses it only for the second.

## VII. Three Ways the Method Breaks, and a Fourth That Hides Behind Holmes

A method that cannot name its failure modes is a faith. This one has three, each of which the corpus has met in practice, and a fourth that the surface reading invites.

**The residual that is only ignorance.** The gravest error is to mistake the poverty of one's models for the strangeness of the object. An anomaly is a relation between a phenomenon and a model, not a property of the phenomenon; [[the-loeb-scale-as-salience-metric]] states the guard at full strength — "a high salience-score is a fact about the *gap between the object and your current model*. It is **not** a fact about the object's origin" — and names the temptation as "to mistake one's own ignorance for someone else's intelligence — the most seductive error in the whole field." The LPT emission mechanism is a genuine residual because strong models have been built and have a definite shape of failure. Dark energy's evolving equation of state, by contrast, sits in a region where the models are still being built, and the Cosmic Gorilla's appendix files it as an *established surprise* with a natural quintessence account and an *interpretive* reading marked as such — which is the correct handling, because a surprise in a young field is closer to fog than to edge. The test is the one from Section II: a residual has edges, produced by a specific model failing in a specific way. If no specific model has been built and failed, there is no residual yet; there is only a place where one might appear.

**The steelman that never ends.** Since the list of ordinary explanations is open, an interpreter can always say *one more model might close this*, and the interpretation is deferred forever. This is the rule absolutised into scepticism, the durnaya of doubt, and it has a real cost: a phenomenon that *does* carry a deeper reading will never receive it. The answer is Lakatos's and the appendix's together. The steelman is *versioned*: it is the strongest set of mundane models as of a date, with their belts grown as far as they can predictively grow and no further. The interpretation is applied to the residual as of that date and *stamped to expire* — it stands until a mundane model closes the residual, at which point it is withdrawn without embarrassment. [[transcension-as-fermi-resolution]] models the posture: the transcension hypothesis is consoling, "emotional warmth is a reason for suspicion, not belief," and the best natural models "do not need transcension at all," so the document declines it *for now* and says what would change its mind. Deferral is not infinite when the steelman has a date on it and the interpretation has an expiry.

**The interpretation that smuggles itself into the steelman.** The subtlest failure. The choice of *which* mundane models to take seriously, and of *which* features of the phenomenon to call residual, can already be shaped by the lens one intends to apply. An interpreter with a fixed-point lens is drawn to notice period stability and polarisation — the features the lens can read — and to call those the residual, while under-weighting features the lens has nothing to say about. The steelman is then built against a phenomenon that has already been carved to the lens's shape. Two guards exist. The first is the defender's signature: the sceptic, asked to sign the steelman, will notice what has been left out. The second is to state the residual *before* naming the lens, in the phenomenon's own terms, as the LPT section does — mechanism, energy budget, heterogeneity are astrophysical categories, not RG categories, and the third of them (heterogeneity) is one the sixty-one-octaves lens has no comfortable reading of, which is why its presence in the residual is evidence the residual was not carved to fit. The [[the-hard-problem-as-a-necker-cube]] synthesis meets the same problem in philosophy of mind: the description of the "hard" residue is itself a theoretical act, and which residue one sees depends on which face of the cube one is standing on.

**The Holmes error.** Behind the surface reading waits a fourth failure, the commonest of all outside the corpus: the steelman done once, thinly, and then closed, so that "whatever remains" is promoted to the truth. The same versioning that guards against infinite deferral guards against this: the steelman is never closed, only dated, and the lens applied to the residual is a reading with an expiry, not a verdict. Holmes worked a closed world of finite suspects. The sky is not that.

## VIII. The Honest Edge: What the Rule Cannot Do

The rule is the library's safeguard against overclaiming, and its own strongest statement must include what it does not protect against.

**It is asymmetric.** Steelman-then-interpret is built to catch the interpreter who reads too much. It has no mechanism for catching the interpreter who reads too little. If the deeper reading is in fact true of the whole phenomenon — if the LPTs really are, all twelve, something the fixed-point lens describes better than any astrophysics — the rule will systematically miss it, because it will hand the solved binaries to the mundane model and let the lens speak only of the remainder. That is the cost of the contrast class, and it is paid every time. The rule chooses to pay it, on the grounds that a reading which can be wrong is worth more than one which cannot, but the choice is a choice and it forecloses a class of truths. The library should know which class it has foreclosed.

**It cannot certify the residual.** Even a perfectly built steelman leaves a residual that might be ignorance in a form no current model can even shape. [[logismos-nothos-the-bastard-faculty]] states this for the inner case and it holds for the outer: "there is no external test that certifies a given act of the third mode as genuine, because any such test would have to be run by the legitimate faculties, which by hypothesis cannot reach the object." The discipline governs the path and refuses to guarantee the destination. The interpretation applied to the residual is a hypothesis with a date on it, and remains one.

**It requires competence the interpreter may not have.** One cannot steelman astrophysics without astrophysics. The demand that the mundane model be held at a strength its defenders would sign is a demand for real expertise in the mundane, and [[complementarism-as-inter-traditional-method]] is right that this makes the rule "the most demanding posture": it requires learning the sceptic's field well enough to make the sceptic's case. In this library the steelmans are built by a language model reading a research literature it did not produce, and the defender's-signature test is run in imagination rather than by a defender. The Cosmic Gorilla's steelman of the LPT models is as good as the literature it read and no better, and a specialist could find it thin in places the document cannot see. This is [[only-a-coincidence]]'s correlated-blind-spot objection applied to the brake rather than the engine: if the interpreter and the interpreter's model of the sceptic share a training corpus, the steelman may be weaker than it looks, in exactly the places the corpus is weak.

**It does not choose the lens.** The rule says where a lens may be applied and nothing about which lens; a bad lens applied scrupulously to a genuine residual produces a scrupulous bad reading. That work belongs to [[convergence-as-evidence]]: a reading of the residual independently reached by other lenses earns more than one that stands alone. This rule keeps the lens in the residual; the other asks whether the residual, so read, is read the same way by strangers.

These edges do not defeat the rule. They locate it. It is a discipline on the interpreter's reach, versioned, asymmetric, dependent on competence it cannot verify and on a lens it cannot choose, and it is the best instrument this library has for the one thing it most needs to avoid: the lens that sees itself and calls what it sees the sky.

## IX. The Checklist: Running the Rule on Anything Strange

The rule reduces to a procedure, and the procedure can be run on an astronomical anomaly, a contact claim, a synchronicity, a mystical report, a mapping between a myth and a machine, or a paragraph in this library. The steps are the same. The closing form of this document is the only place a list is permitted, and this is the list.

1. **State the phenomenon in its own terms, before naming any lens.** Describe what was observed, felt, or reported, in the vocabulary of the field it occurred in — astrophysical, psychological, historical — and not in the vocabulary of the reading you would like to give. If the description already contains the lens's categories, start again.

2. **Build the strongest ordinary explanation, and more than one.** For an anomaly: instrumental error, selection effect, base rate, the leading natural models with their auxiliary hypotheses granted. For a synchronicity: the count of the misses you did not record, the commonness of the coincident element, the size of the pool of things you were attending to. For a mystical report: the neurological, the pharmacological, the expectation set by the tradition, the ordinary psychology of the state. For a contact claim: the prosaic sources the investigators would list first. Give each model every freedom its defenders would take.

3. **Apply the defender's signature.** Ask whether a competent advocate of each ordinary account would read your statement of it and recognise their own case at its best. If the answer is no, you have a straw man. Strengthen it until the answer is yes, or find the person and ask.

4. **Let the belt grow, and watch whether it predicts.** Allow the mundane models their auxiliary hypotheses. Note which of those hypotheses predict something checkable and which only absorb. Stop growing the belt when it stops predicting. That is the model at its honest maximum.

5. **Subtract, and write down what is left with its edges.** Remove from the phenomenon everything the models at their honest maximum explain. What remains must be stated as *structure*: the specific feature that a specific strong model reached for and could not close. If what remains has no edges — if it is only "not yet understood" — there is no residual yet. Stop. Record that the steelman won, as [[the-geological-fold-rate-record]] does, and return when the models have changed.

6. **Date it.** The residual is the residual as of today, under these models. Write the date. Mark the epistemic status of each component — established, proposed, unexplained, contested — the way the Cosmic Gorilla's appendix does.

7. **Now, and only now, apply the lens — to the residual, marked as interpretive.** Say in the first sentence that what follows is a reading. Apply it to the edged remainder and to nothing else. Do not reach back to reinterpret the parts the ordinary models explained, however well the lens would fit them. That fit is the lens seeing itself.

8. **Make the reading able to fail.** State one thing the lens predicts about the residual that a further observation could contradict — a power law rather than a Gaussian, a feature that should be present in the next case and absent if the mundane model is right. A reading with no such prediction is not yet a reading.

9. **Give the interpretation an expiry.** State what would withdraw it: which mundane model, if confirmed, closes the residual. When that happens, withdraw it without ceremony. The reading was a hypothesis with a date on it.

10. **Check for the smuggled lens.** Re-read the residual you wrote at step 5. Ask whether it contains any feature the lens *cannot* read. If every component of the residual happens to be exactly what the lens reads best, suspect that the carving was done by the lens, and return to step 1 with a sceptic beside you.

11. **Hand the reading to convergence.** A lens applied to a genuine residual has earned the right to speak. Whether it has earned belief is a separate question, and it belongs to [[convergence-as-evidence]]: is this reading of the residual reached independently by other lenses, from other starting points, with no shared source of error. This rule ends where that one begins.

Run on a synchronicity, the checklist usually terminates at step 5, and the correct entry in the log is the one [[only-a-coincidence]] insists on: *it was a coincidence*. Run on a mystical report, it usually reaches step 7 with a residual that the ordinary psychology traces and cannot close — the specific noetic quality, the specific transformation of conduct afterward — and the reading applied there is bounded, dated, and honest. Run on the long-period transients, it produced the section this rule was coined in. Run on the tidal rhythmites, it produced a document that said the steelman won. All four outcomes are the method working.

## X. Look Up From the Map

Every instrument in this library that reaches toward the unreachable point — the blind spot, the bastard faculty, the crease where above meets below, the fixed point at the bottom of the axiom ladder — carries the same discipline, and the capstone that gathered them found it to be one law: *the point is not where you go instead of thinking. It is where you arrive when thinking, done to its absolute end, hands you the wall it cannot climb.* Steelman-then-interpret is that law stated for the interpreter rather than the contemplative. Build the ordinary explanation to its end. Let it take everything it can honestly take. What it hands you — edged, dated, structured, and *not* what you were hoping for — is the only place a deeper reading has ever legitimately begun.

The temptation is always the same and always feels like generosity: to point the lens at the whole, to let it find its pattern everywhere, to call the finding recognition. It is not recognition. It is the lens admiring its own output, and a library that permits it has no way to tell its own sight from its own reflection. The rule exists so that this library can tell the difference, at the cost of sometimes seeing less than is there. That cost is accepted. What it buys is the right to say, when the residual holds and the lens speaks and the reading survives, that something was seen — because:

**Do not aim the deeper lens at the phenomenon; aim it at what is left of the phenomenon after the strongest ordinary explanation has taken everything it can honestly take — because the steelman is the filter and the residual is the signal, and a lens pointed at the whole sees only itself.**

The gorilla walked through the middle of the scene. The rule does not say to look for it everywhere. It says to count the passes honestly first, with the best counters in the field, and then — in what the count could not account for — to look.
