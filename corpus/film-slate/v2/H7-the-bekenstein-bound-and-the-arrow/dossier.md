# H7 — The Bekenstein Bound and the Arrow: dossier

## 1. Scope

**The topic.** What the Bekenstein bound is, in the words of the people who stated and proved it; how it relates to the area bounds of black-hole thermodynamics and holography; how Casini's relative-entropy formulation (2008) and the crossed-product regulator of Kudler-Flam, Leutheusser, Rahman, Satishchandran and Speranza (arXiv 2023; *Phys. Rev. D* 111, 105001, 2025) changed its status; where an observer genuinely enters the operator-algebra literature; and whether any of it supplies an arrow of time. The last question is ruled against the standard account of the thermodynamic arrow (the Past Hypothesis; Albert, Carroll, Callender's SEP entry) and against the one place where relative entropy does carry a time direction in this literature: its monotonicity under restriction, which yields the generalised second law (Wall 2011), and the entropy-production identity of Parrondo, Van den Broeck and Kawai (2009).

**v1 films folded.** One: film 78, *The Arrow on the Surface* (wave 4, strand "The Summit"), treatment at `corpus/film-slate/wave-4/78-the-arrow-on-the-surface.md`, which narrated `corpus/synthesis/cosmology/the-arrow-on-the-surface.md` (2 July 2026, ~5,200 words; unedited since the July restructure, commit 28e34a6).

**The three claims under test.** The corpus's own summary of the essay (the vault's MEMORY router, in its row for `threads/arrow-on-the-surface.md`) is: *Bekenstein bound proved = relative-entropy arrow; no-observer-no-surface = the fold; corrects the surface-area slogan.* Each is tested in §5 (rows C33, C47/C55, C58), alongside every other checkable sentence in the essay.

**Out of scope.** The fold as house thesis (H1, Sam's hand): the four-way identity is touched only where the essay makes physics claims on its behalf. The free-energy principle as such (H6). AdS/CFT, Ryu–Takayanagi, ER=EPR and bit threads beyond what the bound needs (the June weed already ruled the AdS boundary condition). The cosmological constant as an information bound (`entangled-measure.md` Part III), noted once in §5 because it shares a document. Biochar, the zero theorem's "framerate", and the bounce cosmology except where they restate the bound.

**One finding framed up front, because it shapes everything after it.** The root essay has never been weeded. The 8 September 2026 weed corrected two of its runners (`the-subtraction-seen-from-two-ends.md` and `the-star-and-the-expander.md`) for exactly the observer claim tested here (log rows 9 and 10), but the root at `the-arrow-on-the-surface.md:157–177, 193, 203, 215` still carries it. Two of the four register notes it planted elsewhere carry its errors as well: the observer claim at `the-windowless-boundary.md:171`, and the arrow and "predictive" claims at `information-geometry-the-fold.md:61`.

**[P1]** J. D. Bekenstein, "Universal upper bound on the entropy-to-energy ratio for bounded systems", *Phys. Rev. D* 23, 287 (15 January 1981), abstract. https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.287 (retrieved 2026-10-14).
> We present evidence for the existence of a universal upper bound of magnitude $\frac{2\pi R}{\hbar c}$ to the entropy-to-energy ratio $\frac{S}{E}$ of an arbitrary system of effective radius $R$. For systems with negligible self-gravity, the bound follows from application of the second law of thermodynamics to a gedanken experiment involving a black hole. Direct statistical arguments are also discussed. A microcanonical approach of Gibbons illustrates for simple systems (gravitating and not) the reason behind the bound, and the connection of $R$ with the longest dimension of the system. A more general approach establishes the bound for a relativistic field system contained in a cavity of arbitrary shape, or in a closed universe. Black holes also comply with the bound; in fact they actually attain it. ## 4. The standard account

**What current physics holds, in brief.**

1. **The Bekenstein bound** (1981) bounds the entropy-to-energy ratio of a weakly self-gravitating system of size $R$: $S \le 2\pi k R E/\hbar c$ [P1, S5, S17]. It contains no area and no Newton's constant [S11]. A Schwarzschild black hole in four dimensions saturates it [P1, S6]. Its derivation from the generalised second law was contested for two decades [S5, S31], and apparent counterexamples were met by insisting that $E$ include every part of a complete system [P9–P11, S19]. Neither the bound nor the holographic bound is close to binding for ordinary matter [S19].
2. **The area bounds** are separate statements. The spherical bound $S \le A/4$ follows from the Bekenstein bound plus gravitational stability where both apply, and is weaker than it there [S7]. The naive spacelike area bound fails for ordinary systems [S2]. Bousso's covariant bound (1999) bounds the entropy on light-sheets [P5, P7, S3]; it is a conjecture, proved classically under hypotheses on the entropy flux [P8] and quantum-mechanically for free fields at weak backreaction [P32]; in weak gravity it implies a (tight) Bekenstein bound [P12, S12]. Its author's emphasis is that it is time-reversal invariant [P6, P7].
3. **Casini's formulation** (2008, building on Marolf, Minic and Ross 2003 [P13, S14]) replaces $S$, $E$, $R$ by the vacuum-subtracted entropy $\Delta S$ and the modular energy $\Delta K$ of a region, and the bound $\Delta S \le \Delta K$ is the positivity of a relative entropy [P16, S15]. For the half-space, $\Delta K$ approaches $2\pi ER$; at the centre of a sphere in a conformal theory it is about $\pi ER$; in general it is non-local [P18, P19]. In this form the bound "holds automatically" and adds no new semiclassical constraint [P16, P24]. The relative entropy itself is defined without any cutoff by Araki's formula [P20, S20]. Longo and Xu gave an operator-algebraic derivation in 2018 [P35]. Kudler-Flam et al. (2023/2025) gave both sides independent definitions with a crossed-product regulator in non-gravitational QFT on curved backgrounds [P40–P43].
4. **Local algebras in QFT are type III₁.** They admit no trace and so no density matrix or von Neumann entropy [S21, P41]; relative entropy is still defined [S20, P29, P31]. In **gravity**, dressing operators to an observer or to the ADM mass turns the algebra into type II and gives entropies equal to generalised entropy up to a state-independent constant [P36–P39]. Which observer is used changes the answer [P45].
5. **The arrow.** The standard account locates the thermodynamic arrow in temporally asymmetric boundary conditions, the Past Hypothesis (Albert 2000 [not fetched], as reported in S25; Carroll's cosmological programme, P15), or in time-asymmetric laws [S24]; how to state the Past Hypothesis in general relativity is disputed (Earman 2006, reported in S25). Statistical-mechanical dynamics is time-reversal invariant, which is Loschmidt's objection [S27]. Where relative entropy carries a time direction, it does so through **monotonicity**: under restriction to a smaller algebra or a completely positive map, which gives the generalised second law on causal horizons [P23, P27–P30, S20], or as the relative entropy between a forward process and its time-reverse, which equals entropy production [P26]. Positivity alone is the static statement used for the Bekenstein bound [S20].

> There is strong evidence that the area of any surface limits the information content of adjacent spacetime regions, at 10^(69) bits per square meter. ## 6. The strongest objection

The objection is made in the words of the physicists whose results the essay cites, with the philosophers of time behind them. It has two halves.

**The bound has no arrow.** The essay's surface is, in its lawful form, Bousso's covariant bound, and Bousso's own judgement of that bound runs the other way: "In fact the covariant entropy bound does not even refer to "future" and "past." The conjecture is manifestly time reversal invariant. We regard this as its most significant property" [P7]. From this he draws the conclusion that its origin "cannot be thermodynamic, but must be statistical" [P5], because "the very concept of thermodynamic entropy has a built-in arrow of time" and the bound has none [P6]. The energy form fares no better. Casini, whose reformulation the essay treats as the decisive step, says that in this form "the bound holds automatically" and that it "does not introduce new physical constraints semiclassically" [P16]. An inequality that every state satisfies at every instant cannot distinguish earlier from later. Casini's own physical reading of positivity is an equilibrium statement: "the free energy of the thermal equilibrium state is the minimum one" [P21]. Witten's review sets out the division of labour the essay misses: positivity of relative entropy is used in "the interpretation and proof [50, 51] of the Bekenstein bound", and "The more subtle property of monotonicity of relative entropy" in the proof of the generalised second law [S20]. The arrow-bearing result in this literature is Wall's: monotonicity under restriction "is the GSL" [P30]. Even there, the direction is supplied by the geometry of a causal horizon, "the boundary of the past of any future infinite worldline" [P28], and by a stationary reference state, not by the asymmetry of the functional. Where relative entropy literally measures the arrow of time, in Parrondo, Van den Broeck and Kawai, it is "the relative entropy between forward and backward states" of a process whose initial conditions (canonical and its variants) are given [P26]. Behind all of this stands the standard account: time asymmetry needs either "temporally asymmetric boundary conditions" or "time reversal noninvariant laws", and "a little analysis of any proposed "third way" arguably proves this to be false" [S24]. The essay's arrow is such a third way. It takes an asymmetry in the two arguments of a divergence for an asymmetry in time, and the sources do not support the step.

**The proof needs no observer, and where an observer does appear it gives more than one answer.** The paper the essay calls "the 2025 proof" introduces its crossed product as "a useful tool in regulating (non-gravitational) QFT", by adjoining "square-integrable wavefunctions on the real line" [P41]. It says its improvement on Casini is "demonstrating that both sides of this expression can be independently well-defined" [P42]. The relative entropy on which the bound rests was already "perfectly well defined, independently of any cutoff procedure" by Araki's formula [P20], and a rigorous operator-algebraic derivation without any crossed product had appeared in 2018 [P35]. Observers enter only in the gravitational constructions: operators "gravitationally dressed to the worldline of an observer" in de Sitter space [P37], or the crossed product by the modular group arising from 1/N corrections in AdS [P36]. Those entropies are defined only "up to an additive constant" [P36, P37]. A 2024 analysis showed that this "procedure depends on which observer is employed", that "the entropies seen by distinct observers can drastically differ", and that the observers in question "are nothing but QRFs", quantum reference frames, that is, physical clocks [P45]. The literature's oldest observer-dependence result, Marolf, Minic and Ross's, concerns the entropy an accelerated observer assigns to an object near a Rindler horizon [P13, S14]. None of these says that a surface has no entropy until an observer is adjoined, or that an observer brings information into being.

**Scope.** Even the original bound is weaker than the essay needs. Page exhibited systems claimed to violate it "by an arbitrarily large factor" [P9, P10]; Bekenstein answered that the energies of "all essential parts of the system" must be counted [P11], and Bousso observes that in this way "Bekenstein risked trivializing his bound, by making it virtually impossible to find a system that would come close to saturating it" [S31]. By Bousso's account Bekenstein privately called the bound a "rule of thumb" [S31]. Marolf and Sorkin showed that existing derivations could be invalidated by highly entropic objects appearing in Hawking radiation [P14]. Bousso concedes that Unruh and Wald "were right": quantum field theory protects the generalised second law without the bound, and "in this sense, the Bekenstein bound was not "needed."" [S16]. And whether black-hole thermodynamics is thermodynamics at all is still argued among philosophers of physics [S26].

**What the objection concedes.** The essay's correction of the area slogan stands (C7, C58). Casini's reformulation is a real and elegant result, and the relative entropy at its core is the quantum relative of the KL divergence the corpus had discussed (C31). There is also a real physics of horizons, observers and a time direction: Wall's generalised second law on causal horizons [P27, P28] and the observer-dressed algebras of de Sitter space [P37]. The essay did not use it.

## 7. Negative space

What the outside sources show matters, and the corpus does not have:

1. **Monotonicity, not positivity, carries direction.** The corpus has no document on the monotonicity of relative entropy under restriction (Lieb–Ruskai; Uhlmann; Araki), or on Wall's 2011 proof that this monotonicity is the generalised second law on any causal horizon [P27–P30, S20, S10]. The arrow the essay wanted lives there, together with a worldline that defines the horizon [P28].
2. **The entropy-production identity.** Parrondo, Van den Broeck and Kawai's exact relation between entropy production and the relative entropy of a process against its time-reverse [P26] is the clean statement that relative entropy can measure the arrow of time. It is absent from the corpus.
3. **The Past Hypothesis.** The corpus never states the standard account of the thermodynamic arrow (Albert; Carroll; Callender's SEP entry; Earman's objection that it cannot be stated in general relativity) [S24, S25, P15]. Its arrow-of-time documents redefine the arrow (`monad-clustering-topology.md:98`) or invoke Prigogine (`time-as-consciousness-technology.md:354`).
4. **Time-reversal invariance of the covariant bound** [P5–P7], which its author calls the bound's "most significant property", appears nowhere in the corpus.
5. **Marolf, Minic and Ross (2003)** [P13, S14]. They are the source of the vacuum subtraction and of the species resolution, and the literature's own observer-dependence result. Casini credits them in his first sentence; the essay does not.
6. **Bousso, Casini, Fisher and Maldacena (2014)** [P32–P34]: the quantum proof of the covariant bound by the same relative-entropy method, which removes the essay's "theorem versus hope" asymmetry.
7. **Longo and Xu (2018)** [P35]: a rigorous derivation four years before the paper the essay credits.
8. **Observer-dependence of gravitational entropy** [P45]. The observer in CLPW is a quantum reference frame, and changing it changes the entropy. This is the most interesting live result near the essay's thesis, and it complicates the thesis rather than confirming it.
9. **The scope debate:** Page's counterexamples, Bekenstein's "complete system" reply, Unruh–Wald, Marolf–Sorkin [P9–P11, P14, S6, S16].
10. **Numerical scale.** The area bound is about 10^69 bits per square metre [S1], and mundane systems fall "well below" the energy bound [S19]. No corpus document that applies the bound to brains, soils, data centres or folding states this.
11. **The two meanings of "holographic."** Bousso disowns the optical sense in a footnote [S8]. About forty corpus documents use only that sense (§2.4).
12. **The QNEC** as the differential form of the bound [S16], and the fact that it was proved several times before 2023.
13. **Philosophical scepticism about black-hole thermodynamics** as thermodynamics (Dougherty and Callender 2026; Prunkl and Timpson 2019; Wallace 2018, via S26). None is fetched here; the SEP pointer is the lead.

## 8. What the sources show

Literal things named in the fetched sources, which a camera could film or a diagram could depict:

- **The gedanken experiment.** A box of entropic matter, a spherical box of radius R, lowered slowly on a string towards the horizon of a large black hole and then dropped in (Bousso 2018 §IV.A, "using a string, say"; Scholarpedia Figure 2, "Lowering the system to the horizon and dropping it"). Bekenstein's 1981 abstract names it "a gedanken experiment involving a black hole" [P1]. Gibbons (1972) doubted that a cord strong enough to hold the box near the horizon is physically permitted; Scholarpedia reports the doubt answered by Fouxon, Betschart and Bekenstein (2008).
- **A thin slab** held with its long side parallel to the horizon, to bring it closer before the drop (Bousso 2018 §IV.B). Width measured "along any spatial direction" [P12].
- **A cavity with walls,** a field in a cavity of arbitrary shape [P1]; negative Casimir energy in a cavity; the rest mass of the walls that must be counted, "a "complete system" that "could be dropped whole into a black hole"" [S31, S19].
- **Page's counterexample apparatus:** an arbitrarily large number of parallel conducting plates; a coaxial cable; domain walls in a scalar field [P9, P10].
- **A photon wavepacket** that could be any of n species, prepared maximally mixed: "we know that a photon wavepacket is present, but not which species it was made from" [S14].
- **Lasers on a flat wall** at x = 0, all flashing at t = 0, their rays forming a null plane that passes through a matter system "like X-rays" and is focused "by an angle" set by its mass; Bousso's example system is "something like planet Earth" [S32]. Light grazing the Sun bent inwards (Bousso 2018 §V.C).
- **Four families of light rays** leaving a two-dimensional surface, two future-directed and two past-directed, at least two of them converging; the converging ones are the light-sheets [P7]. **Caustics,** where focused rays cross and the sheet must end [S3 context].
- **An optical hologram:** "a three-dimensional image is stored on a two-dimensional surface via a diffraction pattern" [S8].
- **The Rindler wedge** and "the trajectories of constant acceleration observers" (hyperbolae) [P18]; an observer "hovering near the horizon of a black hole" [S14]; the Unruh temperature and the thermal atmosphere near a horizon [P25].
- **Casini's Figure 1:** a spatial sphere drawn as an interval, its diamond-shaped domain of dependence, and the curves of the modular flow inside it [P19 context].
- **A de Sitter static patch** with an observer's worldline at its centre [P37].
- **An evaporating black hole** with matter still falling in [P33]; Hawking radiation.
- **Wall's Figure 1:** an eternal black hole with two time slices, each pushed forward onto part of the horizon and all of future null infinity (Wall 2011, Figure 1 caption).
- **A gas** placed concentrated in the middle of a large empty volume, spreading out (Callender SEP §3.2); a film of a process **played backwards** (Frigg and Werndl SEP [S27]).
- **The Earth's surface as an entropy surface:** Bousso's example "a generalized entropy of the Earth's surface", its area plus the entropy of its exterior (Bousso 2018 §III).
- **Scholarpedia Figure 3:** entropy against size for real objects (a neutron star, a globular cluster, black holes) plotted beneath the universal and holographic bounds.
- **Historical settings and people:** Bekenstein, whose black-hole question "was first posed to Bekenstein by his advisor, John Wheeler" (Bousso 2018 fn. 1); the Racah Institute, Hebrew University of Jerusalem (Scholarpedia byline); Centro Atómico Bariloche, Río Negro (Casini's byline); the Jacob Bekenstein Memorial Volume (World Scientific) [S9]; Princeton Gravity Initiative, IAS, Stanford, Urbana-Champaign (Kudler-Flam et al. affiliations); Loschmidt's 1876 objection; Boltzmann; Penrose's estimate of the improbability of the initial state (SEP §3.1).
