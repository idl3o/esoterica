# FILM SLATE — WAVE 5 · "THE OTHER VOICES" (films 88–111)

Twenty-four more films, numbered 88–111 after the 87 of waves 1–4. Every source is a document the negative-space work added against the corpus's integrative turn, and none had been filmed. Each file keeps the slate's format: a treatment, then the line `*Source synthesis preserved below.*`, then the source verbatim. The separator stays even where the source is not a synthesis, because the render pipeline splits on it. Files are copies; the originals are untouched.

> **Why this wave is different.** Each earlier wave had a principle of its own: the spine, the broadening, the lantern, the instrument and the summit. [slate-conclusions.md](../slate-conclusions.md) describes the result as "a single argument delivered seventy times", and until this wave no film argued against the argument. Wave 5 films the documents written to do that: the voices that end without a turn, the four pairs in which a house-style bridge is followed by its native-form companion, and five house-style fills that kept their teeth.
>
> This wave is not the slate's fifth movement. It is not to be written into slate-conclusions.md as the slate's healthy internal dissent, the way *Evangelion* is absorbed there now. That reading would fold the voices back in, which is the move they were written to refuse.

## Decisions, not accidents — do not undo

1. **One writer.** All 24 treatments were written in one pass by one hand. Waves 1–4 were each built by parallel agents sharing one instruction (lock a single recurring image; give every movement a turn), and they converged on that shape. The head-canon pilot of 10 July 2026 found the mechanism that predicts this: delegated agents clump at whatever the shared command names as a target, and only a single mind or a set-level gate spreads them (see [[shared-target-monoculture]] and [vignettes/README](../../vignettes/README.md)). The forms below were assigned by strand, not left to a shared target.
2. **Three forms.** The voices and the four native-form halves get a **card**: casting, mode, what is filmed, and what the film must not do. Every card is shorter than its source. A four-movement treatment above a 117-word document would be the sublimation reflex in the paperwork. The long pair halves get a **treatment that follows the source's own sections** and names what must be left standing at the end. The teeth strand keeps the four-movement form, but each movement names its **tooth**, the fact it must not let go of, where earlier waves named a turn.
3. **Verbatim mode** for the voices and native-form halves. The pipeline's film mode rewrites the text through a model before narration, and a rewrite of "There isn't a turn" will find one. The long halves and the teeth strand use film mode, like every earlier wave, because they are written in the house register and carry wiki-links a narrator cannot speak.
4. **Literal images.** Waves 1–4 asked the image model for painterly, mythic, reverent stills and banned photography. The voices and native-form halves invert that: documentary photographs of the actual nouns, and a negative prompt that bans the symbolic, the glowing and the cosmic. A per-film art direction tells the shot-describer to depict the thing, not a symbol of it. For the four dark voices and *The Wash* it also says never to show suffering bodies directly, only the ordinary rooms and objects around them. Plain light, not beautiful: beautiful darkness still consoles.
5. **No 30-minute floor.** XXII and the native-form halves run from under a minute to seven minutes. *The Plain* says "Do not lengthen".
6. **Plain pace.** The verbatim films run at Kokoro speed 1.0; the house-register films keep the slate's 0.95.
7. **Polyphony voiced.** *The Dialogue* (102) gets one voice per speaker, with a fourth for the stage directions. Read by a single narrator, a polyphonic specimen becomes exactly what Bakhtin called monologic.
8. **No music**, as in waves 2–4.

## Before render

**Claims the films would narrate as fact — weeded 13 October 2026.** A read-through of the house-register sources found six. The reader shared the generator that wrote the sources (standing check 11), so they went to outside sources, and were corrected in the corpus originals and in these copies. The log, with every citation, is [weeding/logs/2026-10-13-wave-5-house-register-claims.md](../../weeding/logs/2026-10-13-wave-5-house-register-claims.md). Three neighbouring overstatements found in the same pass were corrected with them.
- 103 — the LeWitt instruction now quotes *Wall Drawing 86* (1971) exactly; the old wording matched no wall drawing.
- 103 — "R. Mutt" plays on *Mott*, the firm the urinal was bought from; *Armut* is given as a critics' reading, not Duchamp's.
- 105 — Chekhov's 1888 letter to Suvorin is paraphrased without quotation marks.
- 107 — Matzinger's danger model is one influential and still-contested model, not "the deepest current model".
- 109 — Yellowstone is told as a disputed cascade, and Paine's shore carries "with data".
- 111 — the grief motive given to the literalists is cut, and the dispute starts in the generations after Luria rather than in his own circle.

The voices and native-form halves make no checkable external claims.

**Rendering.** The plan is `tools/wave5_plan.json` in text-to-video, built by `tools/_build_wave5_plan.py`. Small pipeline changes support it:
- per-film negative and environment overrides in the wave driver;
- a `prompts.direction` key, which replaces the shot-describer's "striking … evokes" brief for the literal films;
- HTML-entity decoding in ingest, because *The Ladder* carries `&nbsp;`;
- three-digit film numbers in the prep tool;
- `tools/voices_by_speaker.py` for 102's second pass;
- `tools/check_kept_lines.py`, which confirms each film-mode narration kept its refusal line.

A placeholder-image smoke render of 90 ran clean end to end. Its shot descriptions went from five stock-poetic subjects in eight beats (a door ajar, dawn light, a lone chair) with the direction appended, to two in eight with the direction replacing the brief.

Two changes on 13 October 2026, after that smoke render:
- **The shot-describer** moved from llama3.2 to gemma4 (8B). On 76 beats of films 94, 97 and 91 under the literal direction, it wrote 2 stock or symbolic shots to llama3.2's 20, and kept the restraint llama3.2 broke.
- **The twenty photographic films** (88–100, 102–104, 106–109) render on Z-Image Turbo, which in a side-by-side held the literal register with better subject fidelity than SDXL at about SDXL's speed. Turbo runs without a negative prompt, and a named artist printed as text (90's preview drew "STEPHEN SHORE" on a book spine), so their "in the manner of …" clauses are cut in the plan builder. 101, 105, 110 and 111 stay on SDXL with their painters.

Watch the first literal film before trusting the strand.
